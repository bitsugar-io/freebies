LA Freebies: the NES look

Two directions for the LA redesign, side by side: five logo options each, the same four screens in both styles, and a full design system so either one can go straight into the app. Same sample LA deals throughout (Ducks/McDonald's confirmed for 2026-27; Kings/El Pollo Loco unverified).

Chosen: NES-style 8-bit

You picked the 8-bit direction with the B3 pixel-palm icon. I then researched how NES-era sports games looked (full notes: nes-style-research.md) and tightened the style to real NES rules. This section is the new look. The older Jumbotron and 8-bit comparison is still below for reference.

Real NES colorsEvery color now comes from the NES's own 55-color palette, and each sprite uses at most two 3-color palettes plus a black outline. The earlier pastel palette is gone.
FontsPress Start 2P (open license, your pick) for titles, scores, tab labels and cut-ins: short, uppercase, at least 12pt in the app. Body text is the phone's own font so it stays readable and scales with the text-size setting.
NES feel, nothing copiedNo Nintendo name, controller or cartridge shapes, game characters or ripped fonts. Teams appear as plain text, never logos or team colors, the same way Tecmo Bowl named teams by city.

Deal sprites, now live in the API

Drawn to the NES rules (16×16, black outline, light from the upper left, generic food only) and served by GET /api/v1/sprites. Your running app already shows them. These are rendered from the same data.

The new screens (first pass)

Real sprites and NES colors, with your two favorite parts kept: picking teams, and seeing the deals you picked. The full screen-by-screen redesign comes from the design review, which is still running.

Each sport in NES style

How classic NES sports games showed each sport (as references only), and what we take from it. The badges are original 16×16 sprites used in the team picker.

Hockey · Kings, Ducks

White ice with red and blue lines, a scoreboard strip on top. Ours: a "GOAL!" flash on a win, and period lights that fill up.

Basketball · Lakers, Clippers, Sparks

Side-on wood court, taller players, a full-screen dunk cut-in. Ours: a dunk cut-in on a win, and a quarter tracker.

Football · Rams, Chargers, USC, UCLA

Green field with white yard lines, "TOUCHDOWN!" cut-ins, and teams named by city with no marks. Ours: yard-line backgrounds and a touchdown banner.

Baseball · Dodgers, Angels

A view from behind the plate, an inning-by-inning line score, "HOME RUN" fireworks. Ours: the line score shows progress toward the deal (e.g. 5 of 7 strikeouts).

Soccer · LAFC, Galaxy, Angel City

Striped green pitch, a "GOAL!" flash with the net rippling. Ours: a striped pitch background, and women's teams get their own player sprites, not recolors.

Logos, five each

Each is shown as an App Store icon (120px) and at home-screen size (58px and 40px) to check it still reads small. All are generated on a real LED or pixel grid, so they can be exported at 1024px with no redrawing.

32 × 32 LED grid

J1 · LA on the board

Two big lit letters, every unlit LED still visible. The simplest and the most "scoreboard". Strongest at small sizes.

My pick for Jumbotron

A huge W with FREE in green underneath. Says the whole product: win, get free. "W" alone could be any app, though.

J3 · $0 with live meter

Price is zero, the deal meter is nearly full, the red live dot is on. Uses the app's signature component in the icon itself.

J4 · Taco on the board

Food drawn in LEDs, like a jumbotron graphic between periods. Most food-forward; the FREE label gets tiny at 40px.

J5 · F with power meter

A big F (Freebies) with a vertical meter filling green to red, like a crowd-noise meter. Most ownable shape, least obvious meaning.

Rules

  • Doto is for numbers, labels and short headlines only (≤ 3 words). Everything a person reads as a sentence is Barlow.
  • Red means live, nothing else. Green means unlocked, nothing else.
  • Glow only on lit LED elements (score, meter, unlocked). Never on body text or buttons.
  • Unlit dots show the grid; keep them at ≤ 15% contrast so they read as texture.
  • Contrast: amber #FFB000 on #050505 is 11.5:1; dim #9A6A00 is 4.6:1, so only use it for text ≥ 11px.

Components

  • LedText: renders text on a dot grid (SVG), used for the wordmark and icon
  • Scoreboard: teams, score, period/clock, live dot
  • DealMeter: 10 segments; progress from the rule (goals ÷ target, or lead + time for "win")
  • Ticker: one crawl, paused when reduced motion is on
  • UnlockedHero + LedCountdown
  • TeamTile: lit/dim states

Motion and feel

  • Unlock: 3 blinks over 1.2s + Haptics.notificationAsync(Success), once.
  • Meter segments light one at a time (60ms stagger) when the score changes.
  • Optional crowd-roar sound on unlock, off by default.

Rules

  • Colors only from the 16-color palette (a subset of DawnBringer 32, a free palette widely used for pixel art). No gradients, no blur, no rounded corners.
  • Press Start 2P max 3 words, never for body or numbers people must compare. Pixelify Sans for everything else.
  • Pixel art on an 8px grid, always scaled by whole numbers (2×, 3×, 4×) with crispEdges, never smoothed.
  • All sprites drawn for this app. No coins, blocks, mushrooms or ghosts that recall specific games.
  • Game words in moderation: "Items", "Stage clear", "Select". The deal copy stays plain English.

Components

  • PixelBox: 4px stepped-corner border via box-shadow (no images)
  • DealBar: 12-cell HP-style bar
  • DialogBox: text box with bobbing ▼; tap to advance
  • PixelMenu: blinking ▶ cursor rows, 44pt tall each
  • Sprite: renders bitmap strings to SVG (food items, icons)
  • TeamSelectGrid and StageClear

Motion and feel

  • All animation in steps(): 2-frame bobs, 1s cursor blink. No easing.
  • Unlock: STAGE CLEAR drops in over 4 frames + a short chiptune blip (opt-in) + haptic.
  • Season collection: a badge sprite per team per season ("collected 12 deals").

Getting either one into the app

The same steps for both directions; only the tokens, fonts and component skins differ. Branch: feat/la-redesign.

  1. Audit. Run mobile-design-review on the current app to get a baseline (emoji tabs, ~15 hardcoded hex values, no font loading, card-stack screens).
  2. Tokens. Rewrite useTheme.tsx with the semantic tokens above. Jumbotron forces dark (userInterfaceStyle: "dark"); 8-bit can keep a light "day level" later.
  3. Fonts. expo-font with Doto + Barlow Semi Condensed, or Press Start 2P + Pixelify Sans (all Open Font License). Add a type scale token; replace ~48 inline font sizes.
  4. Drawing primitive. One GridArt component (react-native-svg) that renders bitmap strings as dots (Jumbotron) or squares (8-bit). It draws the wordmark, logo, sprites and icons, which is how this page draws them.
  5. Navigation. Tabs become Live/Tonight, Deals, Teams, You with the new icon set (mobile-nav-plan for the route changes; deep link freebie://today kept).
  6. Components. Build the six or seven components listed above as server-driven blocks so /config can arrange them.
  7. Icon and splash. Export the chosen logo at 1024px from the same bitmap; splash uses the wordmark on the background color; add the missing notification-icon.png (white silhouette).
  8. Store. New screenshots from these four screens (apple-skills screenshot-planner), name and subtitle (keyword-optimizer).
  9. Check. mobile-design-review again to compare against the baseline, VoiceOver pass, Dynamic Type at the largest size.

My recommendation

Jumbotron, with J1 as the logo. It's tied to watching the game, which is the moment this product matters, and it leads directly into live alerts and Live Activities. It also works without live data on day one: before live data exists, the meter shows the final score's progress from last night's game.

If you want 8-bit's charm without giving up the arena, a hybrid works: Jumbotron as the app, with 8-bit sprites for the food items (the taco and burger drawn as LED dots, as in J4).

Every logo and sprite on this page is drawn in code from bitmap strings, so whichever you pick can be exported at any size and reused in the app. Fonts: Doto, Barlow Semi Condensed, Press Start 2P and Pixelify Sans, all from Google Fonts under the Open Font License.
